Summary, cheat sheet and references#

You started from a 100 MB CSV and finished with a documented, statistically careful analysis of EEG rhythms. This page collects the take-home messages.

Five lessons of the lab#

  1. Know the generator. Electrode, amplifier, filters and ADC decide what the numbers can contain (Point 1).
  2. Check before you compute. Sampling rate, completeness, units, empty cells and sensor faults (Point 2 to 4).
  3. Samples are not independent, and data are not Gaussian. Use robust statistics, effective sample sizes, log transforms and effect sizes (Point 5).
  4. Frequency is the natural language of EEG. Window, average (Welch), integrate over bands, separate background from oscillation (Point 6 to 8).
  5. Report limits honestly. Percentage of data kept, thresholds, null results and the absence of event markers (Point 9).

Common pitfalls#

PitfallWhy it hurtsRemedy
Computing statistics on the whole fileArtifacts dominate mean, std and spectraEpoch, reject, use robust statistics
Trusting contact quality onlyGood contact does not mean clean signalCombine cq, sq and amplitude rules
Treating samples as independentConfidence intervals too narrow, p-values too smallNeff, block bootstrap, report effect size
No window before the FFTSpectral leakageHann window; Welch averaging
Causal filtering offlinePhase distortion and delayssosfiltfilt
Fixed band edges for everyoneIndividual peak frequencies differLocate the peak, individualise the band
Interpreting relative power aloneShares sum to 100%Look at absolute power as well
Over-interpreting without markersNo condition labels in the fileState hypotheses, avoid causal stories

A word of caution

The definitions of the Emotiv quality metrics given in this lab combine the manufacturer's documentation with observations made on this very file (for example, that sq.*.rms matches a trailing 2-second RMS). When in doubt, trust the data and the current vendor documentation over this page.
